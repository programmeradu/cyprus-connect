Every article, pillar page, glossary entry, learn/documentation copy, and long-form marketing text MUST strictly follow **ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English**.

**Why:** Consistency, clarity, and readability for a bilingual EN/EL audience; reduces ambiguity in regulatory/compliance content.

**How to apply (core STE rules):**
- One idea per sentence. Procedure sentences ≤ 20 words; descriptive ≤ 25 words.
- Use only approved words from the STE dictionary. One word = one meaning, one part of speech.
- Use active voice. Address the reader directly ("You must...", not "It should be noted that...").
- Use simple present tense wherever possible. Avoid past participles as adjectives.
- No slang, idioms, jargon, metaphors, or colloquialisms. No em-dashes.
- Use articles (a/an/the) — do not drop them.
- Use vertical lists for sequential steps or conditions.
- Consistent terminology: pick one term per concept and reuse it (never synonymize for style).
- Warnings/cautions come BEFORE the instruction, not after.
- Noun clusters ≤ 3 words.
- Avoid "-ing" gerunds where a simple verb works.

**Applies to:** src/data/learn/**, glossary, pillar content (EN + EL equivalents), all `/docs`, README-style docs, and any new marketing long-form. Does NOT apply to hero taglines / short marketing headlines where brand voice takes priority.
