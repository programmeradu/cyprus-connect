## 1. Value proposition

Vuneli turns a Cypriot small business's existing records, its electricity bills, fuel purchases and supplier invoices, into a sustainability disclosure that a large customer, a bank or an auditor will accept, with the evidence trail that makes the figure defensible.

---

## 2. Problem and opportunity

Large EU companies must now report emissions across their value chain. They cannot do that without data from their suppliers, and in Cyprus most of those suppliers are firms with ten to sixty employees, one part-time finance person and no sustainability function. The obligation arrives as a customer email with a spreadsheet attached, and the recipient does not know what to enter.

The Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism makes this concrete. Its definitive regime started in January 2026. A Cypriot importer of steel, cement or aluminium now has a quarterly filing with a financial consequence behind it.

Cyprus is harder than the EU average in one measurable way. The national grid runs near 610 gCO2 per kWh against a European average near 230. Scope 2 is the largest line for most Cypriot firms, so a tool that applies a European default understates the result by more than a factor of two. An assurance provider looks for exactly that error.

The current alternative is a spreadsheet prepared by a consultant, quoted in the low thousands of euro, retaining nothing, repeated in full the following year, and usually built on a generic emission factor. We are not asking Cypriot firms to add a cost. We are asking them to convert an irregular consulting cost into a smaller recurring one that keeps the data.

**Unique selling point.** A correct, traceable, Greek-and-English disclosure produced from Cyprus-specific factors, at 540 euro a year rather than a four-figure consulting fee.

---

## 3. Solution

**Built and running today**, not planned: a single-company console with workspace-scoped data; a Cyprus emission factor layer holding grid intensity as a period series rather than a constant; metric ingestion for electricity, fuel, water, waste and travel, each reading carrying its period, source and confidence; a VSME drafting engine that produces the B1 to B12 Basic Module disclosures as a structured, exportable document; a workspace-aware copilot; an audit ledger; seven public compliance calculators; and a library of twenty-five guides and fifty-one glossary terms.

**The approval gate.** The copilot proposes four kinds of action: draft a report, create a task, update an obligation, log a reading. It executes none of them. Each proposal waits for a person. On approval the payload is re-validated against the workspace before the action runs, and an activity event is written with actor, verb, object and time. This is the mechanism that makes a Vuneli figure traceable, and it cannot be switched off by a customer.

**Where the model is used and where it is not.** The model drafts narrative, classifies documents and extracts figures for human confirmation. It does not calculate emissions, does not choose a factor and does not approve anything. Arithmetic is deterministic code. An inspector can reproduce any Vuneli number without running a model.

**Not yet built,** stated precisely: no authenticated feed from the Electricity Authority of Cyprus, bills are uploaded and read by OCR; no production integration with the Cypriot accounting stack; no Greek-language report output; no assurance firm relationship. Each is a funded milestone in section 8 rather than a claim.

**Technological and industrial risk.** The difficult part is not the drafting. It is producing a regulated-grade number from messy Cypriot source documents, with a factor set that is correct for the period, and an evidence chain that survives an auditor's question. That is where the project can fail, and it is where the engineering money goes.

**Climate, societal and gender dimension.** Measurement is a precondition for reduction, not a substitute for it, and we do not claim tonnes avoided per subscription. Our measurable outcome is the number of Cypriot firms holding a factor-correct baseline and a second comparable year: 1,062 by the end of 2028. Societally, a thirty-person Cypriot firm can answer a large buyer without hiring a consultant, which is the difference between staying on a vendor list and being removed from it. On gender we commit to at least 40 per cent of the first ten Cyprus hires, and we report the outcome at re-evaluation. We are two male founders and we do not present that as balanced.

**Why now.** The definitive CBAM regime started in January 2026. VSME reached stable published form. Model inference for the drafting task now costs 26 euro per account per year, which a 45 euro monthly price can carry. None of the three was true in 2023.

**Traction, stated honestly.** The product is built, public and indexed, and it produces organic arrivals. There are zero paying customers, zero signed letters of intent and no completed pilot. The first funded quarter exists to change that.

| Assurance relationships | None yet | Established | Direct |
| Funding and brand | Pre-seed | Well funded | Local trust |

Competitor sites: greenly.earth, plana.earth, sweep.net.

**Where they beat us.** All three cover more frameworks, hold real assurance relationships, and have brand recognition with the large EU buyers who send the questionnaires. For a Cypriot company large enough to need full CSRD-grade reporting across many standards, one of them is the right choice and we say so in the sales conversation. We are also behind on CBAM today.

**The real incumbent** is the consultant with a spreadsheet, not another software product. It wins on trust and loses on cost, retention of data and factor accuracy. We do not intend to displace Cypriot advisers; section 7 makes them our main distribution channel.

**Barriers to entry we are building.** The Cyprus data layer, effective within twelve months: a maintained grid factor series by period, EAC bill parsers, Registrar identifier mapping, and Greek disclosure templates reviewed by a resident practitioner. The accountancy channel, effective from Month 18: a practice that has trained staff and moved twenty clients does not move again for a small price difference. The retained data, effective from 2029: a third-year customer holds three comparable years, and moving means re-establishing a baseline.

## 6. Proprietary technology and intellectual property

No patent is sought. Software patents in this field are slow, costly and weak, and the money belongs in the data layer.

We hold copyright in proprietary source code; the compiled Cyprus factor series and its period history, which is our own compilation work; the Greek disclosure template set; the EAC bill-format parsers; and the Vuneli mark, for which an EU trade mark application is budgeted in 2026.

Qualifying software copyright developed by Cyprus-based staff falls within the Cyprus IP Box regime, giving an 80 per cent notional deduction under the modified nexus approach and an effective rate of approximately 3 per cent after the 2026 rise of the headline corporate rate to 15 per cent. We have modelled no IP Box benefit, because the company is loss-making until 2030 and the figure would be immaterial. It is a reason the R&D is done in Cyprus, not a line of income.

## 7. Business model, pricing and go-to-market

| Tier | Price | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Free | 0 | Tool users, first workspace |
| Pro | 45 euro per month | 10-49 employees |
| Enterprise | 185 euro per month | 50-249 employees, groups, accountancy practices |
| Service engagement | 950 euro | Reviewer-checked VSME or CBAM pack |

Cyprus VAT at 19 per cent applies at checkout. Contracts are monthly rolling by default, with two months free on annual prepayment. We do not lock a thirty-person firm into twelve months before it has evidence we are useful; the cost of that choice is that churn appears immediately, which is the correct trade for a company that needs a true retention signal in year one.

Service attach falls from 35 per cent of accounts in 2026 to 22 per cent by 2030. A service line growing as a share of revenue would mean the software is not working.

**Channel one, accountancy and advisory, from Month 2.** Cypriot SMEs take regulatory advice from their accountant. Several hundred practices exist, and each holds dozens of clients above ten employees who are now being asked sustainability questions. We offer an Enterprise workspace with unlimited client sub-workspaces, white-label output, staff training and 20 per cent of first-year subscription value. Target: eight signed practices in 2026, twenty-four in 2027. This converts a market of 6,194 firms into a market of a few hundred practices, which one Cyprus-based sales lead can cover.

**Channel two, the CBAM wedge, from Month 4.** CBAM importers are identifiable, concentrated in construction materials and metals distribution, and now hold a quarterly filing with a penalty behind it. We estimate 300 to 500 exposed Cypriot firms. The approach leads with the deadline, not with sustainability. The CBAM pack is the entry sale and the VSME workspace is the expansion.

**Channel three, search and content, running now.** Twenty-five guides, fifty-one glossary pages and seven login-free tools, each ending in a workspace prompt. This is why blended acquisition cost falls from 770 to 375 euro across the plan. Greek versions of the top twenty pages ship in Q4 2026.

**Channel four, institutions, from Month 6.** Member briefings with the Cyprus Chamber of Commerce and Industry, the Employers and Industrialists Federation and sector associations. This is a credibility channel, not a volume channel. We have no agreement with any of these bodies today and we name them as intended engagement only.

**The sales arithmetic behind the customer numbers.** 2026: two sellers, 320 qualified conversations, 20 per cent blended close, 64 direct closes; self-serve adds 64 at a 2.6 per cent conversion; total 128 new logos. 2027: three quota carriers, 1,050 conversations, 21 per cent close, 220 direct; self-serve adds 136; total 356. 2028: 1,900 conversations at 23 per cent, 437 direct plus 264 self-serve, total 701. 2029 and 2030: 1,030 and 1,349 on the same basis with Greece opening in Month 9 of 2029. Customer counts in section 9 are net of churn at 2.5 per cent monthly falling to 1.5 per cent.

## 8. Roadmap

**2026.** Q1, EAC bill parsing hardened and the Cyprus factor series versioned by period. Q2, CBAM definitive-regime quarterly filing pack. Q3, per-figure evidence chains and a structured review with one named Cypriot audit practice, whose outcome we report at re-evaluation whether or not it is favourable. Q4, Greek disclosure templates and Greek versions of the top twenty library pages, reviewed by our resident practitioner rather than machine translated.

**2027.** Authenticated data feeds, SoftOne first for Cyprus, then QuickBooks and Xero. Multi-entity workspaces, the technical precondition for the accountancy channel to scale. Supplier data requests inside the product.

**2028 onward.** Sector modules for construction materials and food manufacturing. Bank-facing export formats. Greek factor set and localisation ahead of the 2029 entry.

Each item is tied to a named hire in section 9 and a cost line in section 10.
