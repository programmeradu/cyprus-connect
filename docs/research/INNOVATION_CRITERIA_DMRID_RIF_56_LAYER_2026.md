## Part A — The official definitions (Layers 1–10)

**L1. DMRID definition of an "Innovative Startup".** An unlisted small enterprise, up to 5 years from registration, no profit distributed, not formed by a merger. It "should have and/or should be developing new products/services/processes that create new or disrupt existing markets", based on **new technologies**, **adaptation of existing technologies**, and/or **new business models** [1].
→ Consequence: the visa accepts adapted technology. But the product must *create or disrupt a market*. "A better carbon calculator" does not do this. "A new way to make SME compliance happen without a consultant" can.

**L2. Older DMRID definition (2022).** "A venture that develops new innovations, creating a sustainable competitive advantage in the future." The evaluator still thinks in terms of a **durable advantage** (a moat), not a feature list.

**L3. The R&D-cost test.** The 2019 scheme said a firm is innovative if R&D costs are **at least 10% of operating costs**, certified by an external auditor (Annex IV) [4]. From 2025, firms with R&D above 10% of operating costs (or revenue above €1M) get "tailored evaluation criteria" [5].
→ Consequence: Vuneli must be able to *prove* R&D spend. This needs a cost ledger that separates R&D from operations from day one.

**L4. Frascati definition of R&D.** "Creative and systematic work undertaken in order to increase the stock of knowledge … and to devise new applications of available knowledge" [9]. Three types: basic research, applied research, experimental development. A commercial SaaS startup does **experimental development**, and sometimes **applied research**.

**L5. The five Frascati criteria.** An activity is R&D only if it is all of these [9]:
1. **Novel** – it aims at new findings.
2. **Creative** – it uses original ideas, not routine work.
3. **Uncertain** – the result, cost or time is not known at the start.
4. **Systematic** – it has a plan, a budget and records.
5. **Transferable / reproducible** – the results can be written down and repeated.
→ This is the most important test in this document. Every "research" claim for Vuneli must pass all five.

**L6. What Frascati excludes.** Routine software upgrades, bug fixes, standard integration of known components, data entry, market research, and quality control are **not** R&D. Joining existing APIs and an LLM with standard methods is **not** R&D. The R&D is the part where a *technical question has no known answer*.

**L7. Oslo definition of innovation.** "A new or improved product or business process (or combination thereof) that differs significantly from the unit's previous products or processes and that has been made available to potential users." Innovation requires **implementation** (users can get it). R&D does not [9].

**L8. Novelty levels (Oslo).** New to the firm → new to the market → **new to the world**. RIF and DMRID score higher for "new to the market" (Cyprus / EU) and highest for "new to the world".

**L9. EU GBER research categories.** *Industrial research* = planned research to get new knowledge for new products. *Experimental development* = combining and using existing knowledge to develop new or improved products, including prototypes and pilots. *Feasibility study* = evaluation of a project's potential [10]. RIF budgets and aid intensities use these categories.

**L10. RIF definition of a startup.** "Companies with a technological orientation and potential for high growth, … developing a product/service that the founders … seek to make available to the international market" [6]. Two filters: **technology-led** and **international**. A Cyprus-only market framing is a weakness for RIF (see L48).

## Part B — How DMRID evaluates the Startup Visa (Layers 11–20)

**L11. Goal of the scheme.** "Creation of new jobs, the promotion of innovation and research, the development of the business ecosystem and … economic development" [1]. Every section of the plan should map to one of these four.

**L12. Decision body and time.** DMRID decides within **five weeks** of a complete application [2]. The file goes by email to startupvisa@dmrid.gov.cy [3].

**L13. Application pack.** Annex I (form), Annex II (business plan guidelines), Annex IV (auditor certificate, for firms with history) [3]. Annex II is the structure that the evaluator reads against.

**L14. Scoring model.** Evaluation uses a points grid (Annex III). Vuneli's existing plan lists innovation, business plan, team, and a fourth criterion that is still open. **[UNVERIFIED: exact weights and pass mark. Request Annex III from DMRID before submission.]**

**L15. Innovation proof for a new firm.** For a firm with no financial history, the evaluation depends on the **business plan** [4]. So the plan itself must *show* the novel technical work: problem, unknown, method, milestones.

**L16. Growth potential.** The words "high growth potential" appear in every version [1][2]. The evaluator looks for scalability: a product that grows revenue faster than headcount.

**L17. Local economic benefit.** Jobs in Cyprus, local spending, the 50% cap on non-EU staff, and the €150k investment threshold for more foreign hires [5]. Show a Cyprus hiring plan with R&D roles (data scientist, ML engineer, sustainability analyst).

**L18. Equity and funding.** Founders hold at least 25% (from 2025) [5]. Evidence of access to funds (historically €20k individual / €30k team) [4].

**L19. Renewal conditions.** From 2025 renewal depends on measurable results (for example jobs, revenue, investment, or R&D) [5]. The innovation claim must lead to **milestones we can report later**.

**L20. Ecosystem links.** DMRID values links to universities, research centres, incubators and accelerators. A named research partner (UCY, CUT, Cyprus Institute, KIOS, CYENS) increases credibility.

## Part C — How RIF evaluates (Layers 21–34)

**L21. Programme family.** PRE-SEED (up to €119,999, idea stage), SEED (up to €500k), INNOVATE (existing firms), DISRUPT (up to €1.5M), BOOST (co-invest with private equity, up to €300k), Research in Enterprises [6][7][8].

**L22. Current calendar.** PRE-SEED/0526 closed on **11 September 2026**; evaluation Sept 2026 – March 2027 [6]. The next SEED / INNOVATE calls usually open in May–June. **[UNVERIFIED for 2027 dates.]**

**L23. Evaluation method.** Preliminary eligibility screening. Then remote scoring by **two experts** (SEED) or three evaluators + a rapporteur (Research in Enterprises). Then a **panel interview** with business experts for Stage B [7][8].

**L24. Score threshold.** SEED/0525 required **at least 12.00 / 15.00** to go to Stage B [7]. This means about 4/5 on each criterion. Weak sections are fatal.

**L25. The three criteria (standard RIF / Horizon model).** Each scored 0–5:
- **Excellence** – level of innovation, technical approach, soundness of method.
- **Impact** – market, commercial potential, international competitiveness, social and economic benefit.
- **Implementation** – team, work plan, budget, risk management.

**L26. "High level of innovation and differentiation".** RIF requires products with "a high level of innovation and differentiation, in comparison to existing or competitive solutions" [6]. A direct competitor table against Greenly, Normative, Plan A, Sweep, Watershed and Cyprus consultancies is required.

**L27. State of the art.** Evaluators expect a clear description of the current state of the art, and exactly *where* the proposal goes beyond it. Without this, "Excellence" drops to 2–3.

**L28. TRL position.** RIF innovation calls expect a move from about TRL 3–4 (proof of concept / lab validation) to TRL 6–7 (prototype demonstrated in a real environment). State the start TRL and the end TRL for each technical block.

**L29. Technical risk.** Evaluators reward proposals that name real technical risks and mitigation. A proposal with "no risk" reads as "no research".

**L30. Measurable KPIs.** Accuracy targets, time saved, cost per report, number of pilot users. Numbers must be testable.

**L31. Team.** "Appropriate and complementary technical, scientific and business knowledge" and commitment [6]. A team with no ML or data-science person makes an AI-research claim weak.

**L32. Knowledge transfer.** RIF gives access to the Central Knowledge Transfer Office [6]. A link with a university or research centre shows serious research intent.

**L33. S3 alignment.** THALEIA Priority 1 funds "research and innovation capacities and the uptake of advanced technologies" [7][8]. Cyprus S3 priorities include ICT, energy, and environment / climate. Vuneli sits at ICT × energy × climate. Say this explicitly.

**L34. Horizontal priorities.** Gender balance (SEED prioritises the top three proposals led by women [7]), green transition, digital transition, and SME competitiveness.

## Part D — What "research-worthy" looks like for a software / AI startup (Layers 35–46)

**L35. Integration is not research.** Connecting OCR, an LLM, a database and a PDF export is engineering. Evaluators see this often, and it scores low on Excellence.

**L36. Research = an open technical question.** Example form: "Can we estimate X with error below Y% when only Z data is available?" If a competent engineer can answer it from documentation, it is not research.

**L37. Hypothesis and experiment.** Each research block needs a hypothesis, a data set, a baseline, a metric, and a result that may fail.

**L38. Own data set.** A proprietary, growing data set that others do not have is the strongest moat for AI startups. Example: labelled Cyprus utility bills, Cyprus supplier emission factors, SME activity data.

**L39. New models or methods.** A new algorithm, a new model trained on local data, or a new method for a known problem (for example emission estimation from sparse SME data) counts as novel.

**L40. Verification and trust.** For AI in regulated reporting, evaluators ask: how do you know the AI is right? Methods for uncertainty scoring, traceability and human-in-the-loop verification are a research area on their own.

**L41. Reproducibility.** Documented methods, versioned models, test sets, and published results (technical report, conference paper) meet the Frascati "transferable" criterion.

**L42. Publications and IP.** Evaluators value a technical paper, a dataset release, a patent application, or a registered software copyright. Cyprus IP Box rules reward qualifying IP.

**L43. R&D records.** Time sheets per R&D task, experiment logs, and a separate R&D cost centre. These support L3 (10% rule) and later audits.

**L44. Validation in a real environment.** A pilot with named Cyprus SMEs, a bank, or a chamber (OEB, KEBE) moves the TRL and proves demand.

**L45. Ethics and regulation.** EU AI Act (transparency, human oversight), GDPR, data residency. Evaluators treat compliance with these as part of good method.

**L46. Scalability of the method.** The method must work beyond one customer and beyond Cyprus (other EU islands, Greece, other SME markets), even if the first market is Cyprus.

## Part E — Common reasons for rejection (Layers 47–52)

**L47. "Me-too" product.** The evaluator can name three competitors that do the same thing. Generic carbon accounting fails here.

**L48. Local-only market.** RIF wants international potential [6][10]. A Cyprus-only story is good for product focus but must show an EU expansion path. Present Cyprus as the **first test market**, not the whole market.

**L49. Buzzwords with no mechanism.** "AI-powered", "autonomous", "agentic" with no explanation of *what is new in how it works*.

**L50. No measurable innovation.** No baseline, no KPI, no TRL, no test.

**L51. Team gap.** No technical founder or no person with research experience.

**L52. Budget that does not match the research.** Most spend on marketing, little on R&D staff or experiments.

## Part F — The evaluator's checklist, and where Vuneli stands now (Layers 53–56)

**L53. The ten questions an evaluator asks.**
1. What is new here, compared to the state of the art?
2. Is it new to Cyprus, the EU, or the world?
3. What is the technical uncertainty?
4. What is the method to solve it?
5. How will you measure success?
6. Where is it on the TRL scale now, and where at the end?
7. What data or IP do you own that others cannot copy fast?
8. Who in the team can do this research?
9. Who pays, how many, and is it international?
10. What jobs and knowledge stay in Cyprus?

**L54. Vuneli — honest current position.**
| Area | Current state | Evaluator view |
|---|---|---|
| Carbon calculation, VSME/CSRD reports, PDF export | Built | Generic. Exists in many tools. Low novelty. |
| Cyprus grid factors, EAC tariffs, bilingual EN/EL | Built | Useful localisation. "New to the market", not research. |
| OCR of utility bills | Built | Standard technology. Research only if we build a Cyprus-specific model with measured accuracy. |
| Workspace copilot with approval flow, report agent | Built (early) | Promising. Needs a defined technical question and measured results. |
| Agent workforce (autonomous agents) | Partly built | Strongest candidate. Needs a method, not only a UI. |
| Expert matching, course generation, marketplace | Built / partly built | Business-model innovation. Not R&D in Frascati terms. |
| R&D cost tracking, experiment logs, research partner | Not present | Required for L3, L41, L43, L20. |

**L55. Candidate research directions that fit Frascati (to explore in the next step).** These are directions, not decisions:
- **A. Sparse-data emission estimation for micro-SMEs.** Estimate Scope 1/2/3 with known uncertainty from very few inputs (a few bills, bank transactions), and learn from each new Cyprus firm. Open question: the minimum data for a given error band.
- **B. Verifiable AI for regulated disclosure.** Methods that attach an evidence trail and a confidence score to every AI-drafted VSME/CBAM figure, with automatic detection of figures that need a human check. Open question: can AI drafting reach auditor acceptance rates with measured error.
- **C. Cyprus document intelligence.** A model trained on Greek/English Cyprus utility, fuel and customs documents (EAC, CBAM CN codes). Open question: field accuracy on bilingual, low-volume document types.
- **D. Supply-chain data propagation.** One verified SME data set answers many different buyer questionnaires (VSME, CDP, bank ESG forms) by automatic mapping. Open question: accuracy of automatic cross-framework mapping.
- **E. Multi-agent compliance orchestration with human sign-off.** A measured study of how far agent automation can go under EU AI Act oversight rules before quality drops.

**L56. What must exist before submission (evidence pack).**
1. One-page innovation statement: problem, state of the art, what is new, novelty level.
2. Research plan per direction: hypothesis, method, data, baseline, KPI, TRL start/end, risks.
3. Competitor and state-of-the-art table.
4. R&D cost centre and time logs (for the 10% rule).
5. Letter of intent from a research partner (UCY / CUT / Cyprus Institute / CYENS).
6. Letters of intent from 3–5 pilot SMEs and, if possible, a bank or chamber.
7. Team page that names the technical and research roles, with CVs.
8. EU expansion path (Greece, Malta, other EU SME markets).
9. IP plan (software copyright, dataset, possible patent, IP Box).
10. Annex III scoring grid from DMRID, to write each section against the exact criteria.
